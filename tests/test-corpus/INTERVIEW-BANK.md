## STAR Stories

### Story: $12M Redirected Through Incrementality Testing
**Tags:** #experimentation #impact #leadership
**Source Role:** Senior Marketing Analyst @ Cascade Retail

**Situation:**
> Cascade had no way to measure the true impact of marketing spend. Teams were allocating budget based on platform-reported ROAS and last-click attribution, which over-credited retargeting and under-credited brand spend.

**Task:**
> Build an incrementality testing program to figure out which channels and campaigns actually drove incremental revenue vs. what was just capturing demand that would have happened anyway.

**Action:**
> Designed randomized holdout experiments across 4 channels (Meta, Google, email, SMS). Built the analysis infrastructure: holdout groups, test sizing, statistical methods, executive-readable dashboards. Ran 30+ experiments in the first year. Presented results monthly to VP Marketing and quarterly to CFO.

**Result:**
> Identified $12M in spend with zero measurable incrementality. Redirected that budget to channels with proven lift. The testing cadence (30+ per year) became the standard for all marketing investment decisions.

**Use for:**
- "Tell me about your biggest professional accomplishment"
- "How have you driven measurable impact?"
- "Describe a time you changed how a team makes decisions"

### Story: Pushing Back on the CMO
**Tags:** #conflict #influence #data-driven
**Source Role:** Senior Marketing Analyst @ Cascade Retail

**Situation:**
> CMO wanted to increase Facebook spend by $10M based on Meta's platform-reported performance metrics, which showed strong ROAS.

**Task:**
> Evaluate whether the increased spend would actually drive incremental results, or whether the platform numbers were misleading.

**Action:**
> Presented results from our Facebook incrementality test showing that spend above $60K/day had zero measurable lift. The platform ROAS looked good because Facebook was taking credit for purchases that would have happened anyway. Proposed alternative: allocate $5M to search where incrementality data was positive, and hold $5M in reserve.

**Result:**
> CMO accepted the revised plan. Search spend increased, Facebook stayed flat. Net result: 8% better ROAS on the combined allocation vs. the original plan. CMO now requires incrementality data before any budget increase above $1M.

**Use for:**
- "Tell me about a time you disagreed with a leader"
- "How do you influence without authority?"
- "Describe a time data changed a decision"

### Story: Taking Google Ads In-House
**Tags:** #ownership #initiative #efficiency
**Source Role:** Marketing Analyst @ Westfield Commerce

**Situation:**
> Google Ads were managed by an external agency. Performance was declining, our account manager changed every 6 months, and we were paying $800K/year in fees on top of media spend.

**Task:**
> Evaluate whether we could do better in-house with no dedicated paid search manager.

**Action:**
> Took the account in-house over 3 months. Rebuilt campaign structure (broad match to exact/phrase), implemented Smart Bidding fed by our first-party conversion data from Snowflake, set up automated rules for budget pacing and bid adjustments. Created daily performance dashboards so I could monitor without sitting in the interface.

**Result:**
> ROAS improved 25% within 6 months. Saved $800K in agency fees. We had better visibility into what was working because the data was ours, not filtered through an agency dashboard.

**Use for:**
- "Tell me about a time you took initiative"
- "Describe a process you improved"
- "How do you handle transitioning work from external to internal?"

### Story: Mentoring a Junior Analyst
**Tags:** #mentorship #growth #team
**Source Role:** Senior Marketing Analyst @ Cascade Retail

**Situation:**
> Hired a junior analyst right out of undergrad. She could write basic SQL but couldn't independently design an experiment or present results to stakeholders.

**Task:**
> Develop her into someone who could run experiments end-to-end and present to the VP within her first year.

**Action:**
> Structured a 6-month development plan: months 1-2, pair on experiment design (I design, she executes); months 3-4, she designs, I review; months 5-6, she runs independently with weekly check-ins. Gave her a stretch presentation to the email team for her first solo stakeholder meeting.

**Result:**
> Within 8 months, she was running her own experiment analyses and presenting recommendations to the email team lead. She's now the go-to analyst for all email channel experiments.

**Use for:**
- "How do you develop talent on your team?"
- "Tell me about a time you helped someone grow"
- "What's your management style?"

### Story: Introducing A/B Testing to a Non-Technical Team
**Tags:** #culture #change #education
**Source Role:** Digital Marketing Coordinator @ Nova Brands

**Situation:**
> Nova's marketing team had never run an A/B test. Every decision (subject lines, send times, creative) was made by the most senior person in the room.

**Task:**
> Introduce experimentation without alienating a team that saw testing as questioning their expertise.

**Action:**
> Started small: ran 3 subject-line tests on low-stakes campaigns. Shared results in a casual weekly meeting: "here's what we learned" not "here's what we did wrong." When one test showed the team's least-favorite subject line won by 30%, it clicked. Within 2 months, the team was suggesting their own test ideas.

**Result:**
> A/B testing became part of the team's weekly workflow. By the time I left, every major campaign had at least one test running. The testing habit outlasted me; the team continued running experiments after I moved on.

**Use for:**
- "How do you drive cultural change?"
- "Tell me about a time you introduced a new way of working"
- "How do you get buy-in from skeptical stakeholders?"
